## Point 1 — what "behind the bar" can and cannot mean here

**It cannot mean literal occlusion by the bar surface.** The popout is on
overlay (3) and the bar on top (2), so the popout paints *over* the bar, never
under it. Demoting the popout to level 2 does not fix it either: within a level
wlroots stacks by creation order, and the popout is created per-summon, so it
would still land on top of a bar that has existed since shell start. Demoting
below level 2 would put it under application windows.

**Nor can the surface be pushed behind the bar's body.** `Bar.qml:613-621`
records the measurement: margins are subtracted from the **usable area**, not
the screen edge — `ExclusionMode.Ignore` does not opt out of the bar's own 50px
reservation on this build. `margins.right: 72` landed the surface edge at 2438
(= 2510 − 72), not 2488 (= 2560 − 72). So the popout surface's right edge is
pinned at the slab's flat face, 2502, and cannot reach the 52px behind it.

**So the reveal line is already the bar's face, and the geometry is already
right.** The panel's closed position is one full `panelWidth` to the right of
open — entirely outside the surface, therefore entirely hidden — and it
translates left, the surface boundary at 2502 cutting it. That *is* a drawer
coming out from behind the bar.

**What breaks the illusion is the fade.** `spawnClip.opacity: opened ? 1 : 0`
runs over `emphasizedInDuration` across the whole slide, so the panel is
translucent while it emerges. A solid object sliding out from behind a solid
bar does not fade in — it is *revealed*. Fading it is what makes it read as
"appearing next to the bar" rather than "extending from behind it".

**Action:** drop the fade on the attached slide; keep it unattached.

The split is principled, not a special case. Attached, the panel emerges from
behind an occluding edge, and the edge alone is the reveal — opacity would be
double-counting a transition the clip already performs. Unattached there is no
occluder at all (it drops from the top of the Hyprland windows onto open
desktop), so the fade is the only thing easing it in and must stay. The
dashboard fades for the same reason: it slides from off-screen, where nothing
is behind it to be revealed from.

## Point 2 — the bulge is 6px short, and why it was made short

`_popoutBulgeHalf = PopoutController.openExtent / 2 - Design.edgeBarFilletRadius`
(`Bar.qml:541`). `openExtent` is the panel's own extent (281), so the bulge's
protruding face is 275 — 3px short at each end. The operator sees exactly this.

Round 4 of 260825-pyf made it short deliberately, and the reason is real. In
`edgebarpath.js:186-196` the flat run travels to `xr + f` **before** filleting
back down to `xr`, so each concave shoulder is drawn `f` OUTSIDE the span it is
handed. With the span at the panel's extent the shoulders reached 3px past the
panel at both ends, and in that band the flare's quarter-pipe fill does not
reach — the bulge showed through as a small square tab of bar-coloured pixels.
It was confirmed by controlled comparison: those rows read ~606 (rim blue) with
the bulge on and 132-139 (background) with it forced to zero.

So insetting the span made the bulge's **total disturbed footprint** equal the
panel (275 + 2×3 = 281) at the cost of its **visible face** being 6px short.
That trade is the defect being reported.

**Action — remove the cause rather than paying for it: pass `f: 0` for the
popout bulge, and set the span to the panel's exact extent.**

With no shoulder there is nothing drawn outside the span, so the face is 281
AND nothing overshoots the panel. The concave blend it gives up is not lost —
the popout's two `AttachedCorner` flares already weld precisely those corners,
which is what they exist for. The bulge's sides become a clean butt exactly at
the panel's edges, which is the joint the flare is built to sweep into.

`f` is a per-call parameter of `buildOutline` (`Bar.qml:893`), so this touches
only the popout bulge — the static centre bulge and the two rails keep
`Design.edgeBarFilletRadius` untouched.

**`f: 0` is safe in the solver, checked rather than assumed.** `_arcCentre`
(`edgebarpath.js:56`) is pure midpoint arithmetic with no division, so with
coincident endpoints it returns that point and `_shoulderSweep` matches it
exactly — no NaN, no wrong flag. The emitted `A 0 0 …` with identical start and
end is omitted per the SVG arc spec rather than being degenerate. The Design
invariant `edgeBarFilletRadius + edgeBarBulgeCornerRadius <= edgeBarBulgeExtra`
still holds trivially: 0 + 1 <= 14 at the open depth.
