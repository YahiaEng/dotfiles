Six ways for the desktop to sit still

Omarchy's screensaver is a fullscreen terminal running tte over a text file of block art. This shell has spent a whole milestone deleting external surfaces, so the mechanism does not port — the idea does. Here are six in-process directions for an Aorus idle canvas, every one drawn at the same scale, on the same 2560×1440 screen you actually have. Pick one, or pick several and get a style switcher.

Scale one shared factor, all plates Screen DP-1 · 2560×1440 · 165 Hz Palette live dracula, 19 roles Reference omarchy @ f4378f0d, vendored tte not installed here

What this rests on

The mechanism does not port. The idea does.

Omarchy spawns one terminal window per monitor — class org.omarchy.screensaver, alacritty/ghostty/foot/kitty picked by xdg-terminal-exec — running tte in a loop over ~/.config/omarchy/branding/screensaver.txt. That is a second toolkit, a second process, and a second theming path.

This repo retired waybar, swaync, swayosd, walker/elephant and hyprlock for exactly that reason, and CLAUDE.md states the rule outright: reintroducing one “would re-split the theming pipeline across a second toolkit for no functional gain.” So the screensaver is a Quickshell layer surface, built the way modules/lock/ is built — N operator-selectable layouts behind one key, coloured through Colours.qml, timed through Motion.qml, hot-reloading on file change.

One bonus falls out of that. Omarchy sets its screensaver at 150 s and its lock at 152 s, because launching a terminal window resets the idle timer and the lock's clock restarts. No window is spawned here, so the ladder stays honest.

The identity

AORUS, run through Omarchy's own transcoder

The wordmark below was not hand-drawn. It is magick rendering “AORUS” at pointsize 400 in FiraCode Nerd Font Mono Bold → alpha extract → trim → resize to 78x20! → threshold → PBM, fed through the half-block pairing algorithm lifted from omarchy-transcode-ascii. Same pipeline, same ▄█▀ vocabulary, and it lands at 10 rows × 78 columns — Omarchy's own logo.txt is 10 × 80. 


  

    Five of the six plates below are built on this grid. The sixth deliberately isn't — see S5.

      S1
      

        
Terminal Effects

        
The faithful port. Block wordmark, cycling reveal effects, true black.

      

      
    

    

    

      

        buildable today
        burn-in: safe
        effort: medium
        780-cell text grid
      

      

        What it is. Exactly what Omarchy does, in QML: a character grid holding the wordmark,
        re-revealed every few seconds by a different effect — decrypt, rain, burn, expand, edge-slide. Glyphs
        resolve from scramble to final; each reveal flashes an accent role before settling to onSurface.
      

      

        Why it might win. It is the thing you asked for, and it is the only plate where the
        screensaver-ness is the point. It also carries Omarchy's best idea for free: the art is just text,
        so branding stays swappable (see D7).
      

      

        The catch. Effects are hand-written — there is no tte here to borrow twenty of
        them from. Budget one file per effect family, not one file total.
      

    

  


  
  

    

      S2
      

        
Card Drift

        
The brand mark itself, adrift. Re-tints on every wall it touches.

      

      
    

    

    

      

        buildable today
        burn-in: safe
        effort: low
        uses assets/logo.svg verbatim
      

      

        What it is. The three fanned cards from quickshell/assets/logo.svg — the mark
        drawn for ~/.face on 2026-08-27 — drifting the screen in the DVD-logo lineage. Every wall
        contact rotates the three card fills one step through tertiary → secondary → primary and
        breathes the fan angle. Corner hits are rare and satisfying, which is the entire joke.
      

      

        Why it might win. Cheapest plate here by a distance, and the only one that uses the
        existing brand asset rather than a rendering of the word. It is also the only funny one.
      

      

        The catch. It is a toy. It says nothing about the desktop and never will.
      

    

  


  
  

    

      S3
      

        
Palette Aurora

        
The core value as motion — the whole screen made of the current theme.

      

      
    

    

    

      

        buildable today
        burn-in: safe
        effort: low
        needs a real blur budget
      

      

        What it is. Three slow blurred fields in primary, secondary and
        tertiary drifting over surface, with the wordmark ghosted at 17%. Nothing else.
      

      

        Why it might win. This project's stated core value is “one theme switch re-themes the
        entire desktop.” This is that sentence rendered. Switch to a matugen wallpaper theme and the
        screensaver becomes a different screensaver — no per-style work, no palette mapping, it just is the theme.
      

      

        The catch. A 7 cqw blur across a 2560×1440 surface at 165 Hz is the one plate here with a
        genuine GPU cost. Verify with ShaderEffect or a downscaled layer, not a naive
        GaussianBlur on a full-size item.
      

    

  


  
  

    

      S4
      

        
Constellation

        
Particles wander, assemble into the wordmark, hold, disperse.

      

      
    

    

    

      

        buildable today
        burn-in: safe
        effort: high
        ~320 particles
      

      

        What it is. The same 78×10 grid, but every filled cell is a destination rather than a
        glyph. Points drift and link to near neighbours, then migrate into formation, hold about four seconds,
        and scatter. The wordmark is legible for a quarter of the cycle and absent the rest.
      

      

        Why it might win. It is the most designed of the six — the reveal has a shape to
        it, and the wordmark earns its appearance instead of just being displayed.
      

      

        The catch. Highest effort on the board. A per-frame particle system in QML means
        Canvas (repaint cost) or a ShaderEffect with the targets baked into a texture.
        Neither is a half-hour job, and neither is covered by an existing pattern in this repo.
      

    

  


  
  

    

      S5
      

        
Ambient Clock

        
Not a logo toy. A dim glanceable screen that slowly wanders.

      

      
    

    

    

      

        buildable today
        burn-in: safe by design
        effort: low
        reuses lock-screen backends
      

      

        What it is. Time, date, weather and now-playing at ambient brightness, with the mark
        small in the corner — and the whole block slowly translating on a multi-minute cycle so no pixel is ever
        static. This is a screensaver in the literal sense, and a useful screen at four paces.
      

      

        Why it might win. It is the only plate you would actually read. Every backend it
        needs already exists and is already wired into the lock screen —
        MediaBackend.qml, weatherBackend, LockClock.qml. Lowest new code of
        the six.
      

      

        The catch. It has nothing to do with Omarchy, and it half-duplicates the lock screen you
        shipped this morning. If you take this one, the honest question is whether the lock screen should simply
        appear sooner.
      

    

  


  
  

    

      S6
      

        
Edge Rail

        
The shell's own edge language, run around a black screen.

      

      
    

    

    

      

        buildable today
        burn-in: safe
        effort: low
        extends GradientBorder
      

      

        What it is. A lit trace running the screen perimeter with a fading gradient tail, over a
        black field with the wordmark quiet at centre. The stroke is the same accent sweep
        GradientBorder already draws around the dashboard.
      

      

        Why it might win. It is the only plate that looks like this shell rather than
        like a screensaver in general — same rim, same sweep, same accent stops, on a surface a hundred times
        larger. Continuity, for free.
      

      

        The catch. Very little happens. As the only style it will get old; as one of two or
        three in a rotation it is the calm one.

Rulings I should not make alone

    
Seven decisions that are not visual

    
Each one is preset to my recommendation. Change any of them and the brief updates.

  


  

    
D1
When does it appear?

    

      Earlier means you see it more; later means it never interrupts a pause in reading. 180 s clears the
      120 s bar-hide by a comfortable margin and still gives two minutes of screensaver before the dim.
    

    

      
      
      
      
    

  


  

    
D2
True black, or the theme's surface?

    

      Omarchy forces #000000 with an OSC escape. True black is what makes a glowing wordmark read,
      and costs least on an OLED — but it is a hardcoded literal, and colour-lint rejects those in
      QML. Taking it means one annotated exception; taking surface means the plates look about 12%
      milkier than they do above.
    

    

      
      
      
    

  


  

    
D3
What dismisses it?

    

      hypridle's on-resume fires on any input the compositor sees, focus or not — so the cheap path
      is to let hypridle hide it and add a local key/motion handler as a second line. The risk of “any input” is
      dismissing it with a stray desk bump; the risk of “keys only” is a mouse-only user stuck staring at it.
    

    

      
      
    

  


  

    
D4
When must it stay away?

    

      Gaming mode already freezes hypridle outright (pkill -STOP -x hypridle), so that case is
      covered with no new code. The open one is media: a paused-looking player is not the same as watching a
      film with the mouse untouched for four minutes.
    

    

      
      
      
    

  


  

    
D5
One screen or all of them?

    

      This host has one output, so the choice is insurance for a laptop or a future second monitor. Independent
      surfaces look better; a shared clock means two screens showing the same frame of the same effect.
    

    

      
      
      
    

  


  

    
D6
Is the wordmark swappable?

    

      Omarchy's best structural idea: the art is a plain text file, and any SVG or PNG can be transcoded into it.
      We already have magick, and the awk half of their transcoder is fifty lines. The cost is one
      more script and one more settings row.
    

    

      
      
      
    

  


  

    
D7
One style, or a switcher?

    

      The lock screen carries five layouts behind one layoutKey and a settings picker; the same
      shape costs almost nothing extra here once the surface exists. A rotation is the third option — a
      different style each time the screen goes idle.
