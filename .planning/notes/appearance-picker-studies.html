The measurement that shapes everything below

Both lists are longer than the number of things you can actually tell apart

This turned up while checking whether a preview grid was even worth building. It is the same defect on both axes, and it is the reason a straight port of the scripts into QML would be a wasted surface.

ICONS Three of the eight themes render an identical grid

Papirus, Papirus-Dark and Papirus-Light are byte-identical at every 48×48 icon probed — same MD5 for folder, utilities-terminal, user-trash and system-file-manager. The two variants override only small glyphs: Papirus-Dark ships its own 16/18/22/24px actions, devices, places; Papirus-Light ships its own 16/22/24px panel and animations. Neither touches 48px at all.

A picker that previews app icons at 48px — the obvious design — therefore shows you three cells that look exactly the same and gives you no way to choose between them.

FONTS A third of the 39 entries are metrically identical twins

The 39 names are 13 families × three cuts: Nerd Font, Nerd Font Mono, Nerd Font Propo. Measuring glyph advance widths straight out of the TTFs across six families — FiraCode, JetBrainsMono, Hack, Iosevka, CaskaydiaCove, MesloLGS — Nerd Font and Nerd Font Mono came back identical on every probe: Latin A and the icon glyphs all sit on the same single cell.

Only Propo diverges, and only on the icon glyphs: it widens them to 0.92em against the cell's 0.5–0.62em, while leaving Latin text untouched. So the choice that matters is 13 families × two spacing behaviours, presented today as a flat 39-row list in which 13 rows are a distinction you cannot see.

fontTools hmtx/head, advance ÷ unitsPerEm, code points U+0041, U+F07B, U+E0B0, U+F0A0, U+E30D, across 6 of 13 installed families. fontconfig agrees: Propo reports no spacing value where the other two report 100.

Five directions

Where the picker should live

Each is drawn to scale against the shell's own surface language — the dark ground, the 12px panel radius, the accent from the live Dracula palette. Fonts in the specimens are the real installed families, rendered here in their web cuts.

two launcher routes

05

Split

deliberately asymmetric
window + route

The premise of the other four is that icons and fonts are the same problem. The measurements say they are not. Icons are 8 items that differ in images, need a fallback chain, coverage flags and a catalogue installer — that is a window. Fonts are 13 families that differ in text you are about to type in, and want a live specimen you can type your own string into — that is a launcher route. So build each the shape its own data asks for.

Before any of this can be bound

Super+I and Super+F are both already taken

Neither is free, and one of them is not really available.

Free slots on this machine: Super+ G H J K O U, and Super+Shift+ A D E H I J L M N O R U V W Y. Three packages fall out of that.

Package A — literally as asked

Super+I icons, Super+F fonts. Costs two relocations, and one of them is fullscreen — it would have to move to a free key like Super+G. Not recommended: fullscreen is the most reflexive bind on the machine and the mnemonic is not worth retraining it.

Package B — the mnemonic without touching fullscreen

Super+I icons and Super+Shift+F fonts. Code editor moves to Super+Shift+I (free, adjacent, one shift away from where it was); maximize moves to Super+Shift+M (free). Super+F stays fullscreen. Two relocations, neither of them reflexive.

Package C — nothing moves

Super+Shift+I icons, Super+Shift+U fonts — both free today. Or simply keep Super+Shift+Z and Super+Shift+X pointed at the new surfaces, which costs nothing and requires learning nothing. Zero risk, weakest mnemonic.

Recommendation

Specimen, with keybind Package B. It is the cheapest direction that fixes both measured defects, and it is the only one whose dismissal, focus grab, motion and route table are already written and already debugged — the wallpaper picker is the same surface doing the same job, so this is closer to a third instance of an existing pattern than a new build.

The catalogue installer is the one thing Specimen cannot host, and it should not be smuggled in: browsing and installing new icon themes is already a standing v5.0 candidate in ROADMAP.md as ICON-BROWSE. Leaving it out here keeps this task small and leaves that milestone item intact rather than half-eaten.

If the catalogue matters more than the cost, Atelier is the direction that earns its price — but it is a workbench-sized build for eight themes and thirteen families, and that ratio is worth being honest about before starting.
