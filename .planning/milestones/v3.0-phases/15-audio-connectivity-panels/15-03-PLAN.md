<objective>
Turn 15-02's one proven panel into three. The wifi and bluetooth panels mount as
instances of the shared `PanelDialog`, each carrying its real Advanced target and
its honest off / no-hardware state, and the shell root gains the single
`IpcHandler` every remaining entry point calls.

This is a real capability delta, not scaffolding. At the end of this plan a
person can open a themed wifi panel and a themed bluetooth panel, see truthfully
why each one is off when it is off, turn back on the one that can be turned back
on, and hand off to `nm-connection-editor` or `blueman-manager` for everything
past the panels' deliberately limited scope. Three `quickshell-*-panel`
namespaces exist and the family layer treatment covers all three.

Two things make this more than mounting work. First, D-15-26's grammar is the
whole point: the difference between "Wi-Fi is off, here is the button" and
"Wi-Fi is off, a hardware switch did it and no button will help" is the
difference between a panel that respects the user and one that lies to them —
and on this host neither unfixable branch can be produced by real hardware, so
each is proven by a named seam, observed, and reverted. Second, the `IpcHandler`
is the seam through which every non-keybind entry point in this phase reaches a
panel; routing both its verbs through 15-02's single guarded `openPanel()` is
what keeps DASH-08's fullscreen refusal to exactly one implementation instead of
three (Flagged Assumption 2 of the outline — `15-PATTERNS.md` is wrong on this
point and must not be followed).

Purpose: PANEL-03 and PANEL-04's surfaces exist and are reachable; PANEL-05's
other two Advanced buttons ship; PANEL-06 becomes structurally true across three
instances.
Output: `WifiBackend.qml`, `WifiPanel.qml`, `BluetoothBackend.qml`,
`BluetoothPanel.qml`, four new `qmldir` registrations, two shell-root loader and
backend pairs, the `IpcHandler`, and two per-namespace layer rules.
</objective>
